# Counterexample audit

A completed review can overlook feedback because it rejected the wrong claim, applied an exclusion too broadly, or accepted a local tradeoff without comparing the whole policy. The final gap pass challenges those decisions; it does not assume that every rejection was wrong.

Give the fresh reviewer the fixed source, requirements, matrices, and the actual reasons for consequential clean, retained, disproven, out-of-scope and unproved conclusions. Include product clarity and code-health decisions as well as safety. Prioritize conclusions whose failure changes the verdict or erases actionable feedback. Keep positive findings out of this brief so the audit does not become confirmation of the existing list.

For each selected conclusion, record:

- The exact proposition rejected or retained, and the evidence offered for it.
- The narrowest useful competing claim. A qualifier may disprove an absolute promise while leaving misleading primary guidance; a safeguard may prevent corruption while leaving the operation stuck. Evaluate the audience and conditions of that narrower claim.
- A concrete counterexample or alternative using the actual callers, transitions and consumers. A scope exclusion applies only when its trigger and effect match this path. For repeated logic, compare the cost and ownership of changing the policy everywhere with a single owner; short code alone does not establish a better design. For local readability, compare the actual statements and logical phases, not only the surrounding architecture.
- The remaining premise and the cheapest decisive source check or probe. An unexecuted complete causal chain is different from a missing causal premise. Follow the coordinating fact-check policy for its disposition.
- The result: keep the original conclusion with its bounded justification, retain a narrower actionable finding, or name the exact unresolved question and its consequence. Preserve a discovered trigger and consequence through final synthesis rather than reducing it to a topic label.

Return counterexamples and concrete coverage gaps, with source anchors and evidence limits. Do not invent obligations to manufacture findings. The coordinator checks the strongest evidence on both sides and owns the final disposition.
